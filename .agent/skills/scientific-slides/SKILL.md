# Scientific Slides

## Purpose

Create a deck that helps a live audience understand the research question,
method, main results, and implications. The editable deck and usable PDF are the
product.

## Workflow

1. Fix the audience, talk type, duration, and one takeaway.
2. Select only the results, figures, and citations needed for that takeaway.
3. Build the shortest coherent story: problem, gap, approach, result,
   interpretation, limitation, close.
4. Give each slide one main idea and one necessary visual or concise text block.
5. Adapt figures for projection only where labels or panel density prevent
   understanding.
6. Produce an editable deck and PDF.
7. Open the deck once and inspect the affected slides for factual correctness,
   overflow, unreadable text, and broken assets.
8. Adjust timing or layout only when it harms the talk.

## Output Contract

Produce:

- editable slide source (`.pptx`, `.tex`, or another requested format);
- a presentation PDF when practical;
- concise speaker notes only when requested or necessary;
- citations for external facts actually shown.

A slide-by-slide audit log, theme report, timing matrix, thumbnail package, or
multiple visual variants is not required.

## Boundaries

- Do not begin with palette, typography, templates, slide-per-minute formulas, or
  decorative layout before the scientific story is fixed.
- Do not force every slide to contain a figure when a short statement, equation,
  or transition is clearer.
- Do not require every result to appear; select the evidence needed for the talk.
- Do not fabricate results, citations, diagrams, or quotes.
- Use Python or presentation tools to create or inspect the actual deck, not to
  generate quality scores, exhaustive issue logs, or proof-of-work.
- Do not run repeated rasterization and visual-review loops without a concrete
  defect.
- Stop when the deck is scientifically correct, readable, and fits the stated
  duration; do not continue styling for its own sake.
